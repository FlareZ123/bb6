# Agent 1: uniform reset-depth bound from short subwords

The reset-subword scan now also records the earliest end-exclusive position of any common reset subword. Across all 9,968 root words n=33..10,000, the maximum is 13 (unique case n=6,932). Distribution:
3: 2,175; 4: 4,214; 5: 2,252; 6: 894; 7: 272; 8: 86; 9: 49; 10: 16; 11: 5; 12: 4; 13: 1.

Therefore, in the finite scan, after at most 13 symbols the carry state has synchronized for both branches, independently of all 9 previous digit pairs and all allowed initial carries. Hence the remaining output suffix is independent of the low carry.

This suggests inflating the 3-adic low buffer by a fixed 13 digits to expose a deterministic high-digit suffix. Caveat: that suffix is not itself guaranteed to contain another reset word, and the next state's window boundaries move. I tested the naive closure and found counterexamples, so this is a route to investigate, not a proof.
