# Growth restrictions on regular-language invariants for Sheep

**Main result.** For every polynomial-growth regular language of canonical binary integers and every fixed valuation bound A, there is a finite H and a finite threshold X such that no genuine Sheep section path of H returns, starting at x >= X, stays in the language with all source valuations <= A. This is an all-starts finite-corridor obstruction, not merely a bound on the size of an automaton. In particular, no nonempty polynomial-growth regular language of integers >=5 can be terminal-free and forward closed under the exact Sheep section map. Combining this with the earlier general zero-transient theorem gives a strict intermediate exponential-growth restriction on any hypothetical regular invariant containing the authentic seed.

The finite-corridor proof uses the classical Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt multiplicative-group unit equation theorem. The raw affine and residue-injectivity lemmas are locally kernel-checked against the unchanged SheepEven.Step relation. The regular-language and number-theoretic synthesis is a written proof, not a Lean theorem. This does not decide Sheep or BB(6).

## 1. Exact section map and sparse languages

For x=2^a u, u odd, x>=5, the exact nonterminal map is F(x)=x+a+3 for u=3 and F(x)=x+a+(u-1)/2 for u>=5. The other possibility u=1 is a terminal power. The authentic blank entry is 5; the already source-certified equivalence is in ../agent16-sheep-even-return/README.md (CI 41505950547). Every real return increases x. If all source valuations are <=A and the start exceeds 3*2^A, every edge is generic, and its affine formula is

    F(x) = r_a*x + a - 1/2,
    r_a = (2^(a+1)+1)/2^(a+1).

Let L be a regular language of canonical LSB-first binary words, with at most polynomially many words of length n. Trim a recognizing DFA to productive states (reachable from the start and able to reach acceptance). Each cyclic strongly connected component must be a simple directed cycle: if a state has two distinct internal outgoing edges, they start return words with different first letters. Raising those words to suitable positive powers produces two distinct equal-length return words. Their arbitrary concatenations, with fixed entry and exit words, produce 2^k distinct accepted words at one length linear in k, a contradiction. A productive SCC with at most one internal outgoing edge at each state is a simple cycle or an isolated vertex. Traversing the finite SCC DAG thus expresses L as a finite union of languages of the form

    w_0 v_1^* w_1 ... v_k^* w_k,

where all words are fixed and each v_i is nonempty. There are finitely many choices of entry, exit, and partial-cycle words, so the union is finite.

For a fixed word z beginning at digit position p (LSB position zero), its contribution is value(z)*2^p. For a repeated word v of length ell beginning at p and repeated k times, the contribution is

    value(v)/(2^ell-1) * (2^(p+ell*k)-2^p).

Consequently there are a finite set C of nonzero signed rational coefficients and a finite M such that every represented integer x of bit length n has an expression

    x = sum_{i=1}^m c_i*2^e_i,
    m<=M, c_i in C, 0<=e_i<=n.                  (1)

The coefficients and term bound are fixed by L. Terms with coefficient zero may be omitted.

## 2. A finite limit-class lemma (S-unit theorem)

Fix A, C, M. For any positive rational alpha, let G be the multiplicative subgroup of Q^* generated by alpha, 2, and the A+1 odd integers 2^(a+1)+1 for 0<=a<=A. It has rank at most A+3. Among elements b>0 of G admitting a representation b=sum c_i*2^d_i with c_i in C, at most M terms, and arbitrary integer d_i, there are only finitely many classes modulo multiplication by powers of 2.

Proof: divide by b to obtain sum c_i*(2^d_i/b)=1. Choose an inclusion-minimal subset of terms still summing to 1. No nonempty subsum vanishes, since it could then be removed. For each of the finitely many coefficient tuples, the ratios form a nondegenerate solution in G^m. The Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem bounds the number of such solutions by a number depending only on m and the rank of G^m (at most m(A+3)). Every solution determines the class of b modulo 2^Z from its first coordinate 2^d_1/b. Thus the number of classes is bounded uniformly over alpha.

For example a uniform bound K is the sum over 1<=m<=M of |C|^m times the ESS bound for m variables and rank m(A+3), taking the one-variable bound to be 1. No practical magnitude is asserted. Reference: J.-H. Evertse, H. P. Schlickewei, W. M. Schmidt, *Linear equations in variables which lie in a multiplicative group*, Annals of Mathematics 155 (2002), 807–836, Theorem 1.1, https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04 (DOI 10.2307/3062133).

## 3. Uniform corridor theorem

Suppose there are arbitrarily large starts of length-K genuine paths entirely in L with every source valuation <=A, where K is the uniform class bound above. Choose a sequence of such paths indexed by j, whose starts x_{j,0} tend to infinity. Pass to a subsequence with one fixed length-K valuation word (there are only (A+1)^K possibilities). Let n_j be the initial bit length. After taking a further subsequence, x_{j,0}/2^n_j converges to some alpha in [1/2,1].

At each fixed return r, affine iteration gives

    x_{j,r}/2^n_j -> b_r = alpha * product_{i<r} r_{a_i} >0.

The fixed additive offsets vanish in the limit. In particular the bit length of x_{j,r} is <=n_j+O_r(1). Apply (1) to x_{j,r}. After a subsequence, the number of terms and their coefficients are fixed; each integer exponent e_i-n_j is bounded above. A further subsequence makes each such exponent either constant or tending to minus infinity. Taking the real limit yields b_r as a sum of at most M terms c_i*2^d_i with integer exponents and c_i in C. At least one term survives because b_r>0. In particular alpha=b_0 is rational. These subsequences may be chosen separately for each r because the b_r limits already exist.

All K+1 values b_r belong to the group G of section 2 with generator alpha. Their classes modulo powers of 2 are distinct: removing the dyadic factor from a positive rational, the odd part of b_{r+1} equals the odd part of b_r times 2^(a_r+1)+1 >=3. This contradicts the bound of K classes. Therefore the set of starts of length-K bounded-valuation corridors in L is bounded, establishing the asserted finite threshold.

The proof needs neither forward closure of L nor a density statement about prepared halting states. It applies to any sparse regular set, and more generally to sets having the bounded signed-power representation (1) with exponents bounded above by bit length plus a fixed constant.

**Effective threshold.** For any fixed path length H and bound A, its allowed starting states form a regular language: fix the finite valuation word, impose membership of each x_i in L, and use the integer affine equations and residue conditions for each edge. A finite synchronous LSB-first carry automaton recognizes each fixed linear equation (including constant multiplication), with high zero padding; intersect the automata and project away intermediate states, then restore canonical representations. The generic cofactor lower bound and starting threshold are fixed inequalities. Take the finite union over valuation words. Finiteness of the result is decidable by the productive-cycle test and, when finite, the maximum can be extracted. Searching H=1,2,... for the first finite start language terminates by the corridor theorem. This is an effective obstruction algorithm, not an efficient numerical cutoff for the true root.

## 4. Excluding all polynomial-growth forward invariants without density assumptions

Suppose a polynomial-growth DFA language I of integers >=5 is nonempty, terminal-free and forward closed. Let t be the length of the transient before the zero-transition orbit from the initial state enters its cycle. Let T be a global transient threshold for the zero action on all states and p a common period of its cycles.

If I accepts x=2^a*u with a>=t, u odd>=3, then by initial-zero pumping it accepts u*2^e for all sufficiently large e=a mod p. Its successor is y_e=u*2^e+e+c, where c=3 for u=3 and c=(u-1)/2 otherwise. Set delta=e+c, and let ell be its bit length. For sufficiently large e, the canonical word is the low block delta, a gap of e-ell zeroes, then u. The zero action permits replacing the gap by b-ell whenever b=a mod p and b-ell>=T (and e-ell>=T). Thus u*2^b+delta is also accepted.

For each sufficiently large fixed b=a mod p, allow delta in [2^(b-T-2),2^(b-T-1)) with delta=a+c mod p. All corresponding e=delta-c are sufficiently large; the required gap inequalities hold. There are on the order of 2^b distinct accepted numbers, all of the same bit length b+bitlength(u). This contradicts polynomial word growth. Therefore every accepted x has a<t. (For t=0 no nonterminal accepted x is possible.) This pumping argument is the elementary part of ZERO-TRANSIENT.md; here the polynomial-growth hypothesis directly supplies the contradiction, without invoking prepared-halting density.

Since I is terminal-free and forward closed, each member generates an infinite increasing exact path inside I, with valuations bounded by t-1. Eventually the orbit exceeds the corridor threshold of section 3. Contradiction. In particular I cannot contain the authentic seed 5.

## 5. Upper growth restriction for a general regular invariant

For arbitrary regular terminal-free forward-closed I, the previous general zero-transient theorem (ZERO-TRANSIENT.md) gives a<t, using Agent17's mathematical prepared-halting residue/phase density theorem. Thus if I is nonempty, t>=1 and A=t-1 is an all-state valuation bound. The following arithmetic estimate is independent of the density argument once A is given.

For a generic valuation word w=(a_0,...,a_{r-1}), let c(w)=sum(a_i+1), N(w)=product(2^(a_i+1)+1). For any two genuine generic paths with the same word from x to y and x' to y', affine cancellation gives

    2^c(w)*(y-y') = N(w)*(x-x').

Since N(w) is odd, x=x' mod 2^c(w). The locally checked theorem path_injective_below_precision therefore gives at most one start below 2^c(w) for a given word. This is the raw-return version of the dyadic cylinder mechanism, complementary to Agent22's even-return Bernoulli coding.

For sufficiently large n, every n-bit accepted x exceeds 3*2^A, so all its subsequent returns are generic. Take the shortest prefix with c(w)>=n; then n<=c(w)<=n+A. If f(c) is the number of words of cost c on 0..A, f(0)=1, f(c)=0 for c<0, and

    f(c)=sum_{k=1}^{A+1} f(c-k)   (c>0).

Let lambda_A be the unique positive root >=1 of sum_{k=1}^{A+1} lambda_A^(-k)=1. For A=0 it is 1, and for A>=1 it is strictly between 1 and 2. Induction gives f(c)<=lambda_A^c. Each word of cost c>=n allows at most one n-bit start. Consequently the number of accepted n-bit words is at most

    sum_{c=n}^{n+A} f(c) <= (A+1)*lambda_A^(n+A).

Write lambda(I)=limsup_{n->infinity} |I at bit length n|^(1/n). For a regular language, either it has polynomial growth or its productive graph contains two branching equal-length return words as in section 1, which implies lambda(I)>1. Section 4 excludes the first option. Thus any hypothetical nonempty terminal-free regular forward invariant satisfies

    1 < lambda(I) <= lambda_(t-1) < 2.

In particular it cannot have full binary exponential rate or positive natural density. The same prefix-count bound limits the upper box dimension of the 2-adic closure of its unbounded part to log2(lambda_A), since each word fixes at most one residue modulo 2^n, even if the start exceeds 2^n. More generally this residue bound applies to any set of arbitrarily large states whose entire subsequent path has source valuations <=A.

The growth upper bound uses the earlier paper-level prepared-density input for the general-DFA valuation restriction; the sparse-language exclusion itself does not.
