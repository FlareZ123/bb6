# Checkpointed SymRunEngine state replay: F424 and F412

**Status: reproducible computational evidence; source-level exact-cost bridge in progress, not yet Lean-compiled.** The replay independently translates the upstream `SymRunEngine.stepSym` transition into JavaScript, executes every macro transition, and compares the resulting state against every source checkpoint. It does not by itself calculate primitive TM time or prove a halting-time bound.

## Formal cost boundary: `stepSym` is not `stepPure`

The upstream halting checkpoints are generated by `stepSym` (the `stepSym16`, `stepSym256`, and `stepSym4096` definitions), not by `stepPure`. The current Agent 11 `SourceCostModel.costPure` and `costIter_exact_reaches` development prices iterations of the distinct `stepPure` function. It cannot be substituted directly for the checkpoint chain.

This distinction has a concrete separating state:

```lean
s := (.BB, [.repB1 1], [])
stepPure s = (.BB, [], [.repB1 1])
stepSym s  = (.CB, [], [.item (.run 1), .item (.run 1)])
```

The direct `stepSym` replay charges 11 for the B1 sweep and 1 for the terminal empty-left BB transition, total 12; `costPure` charges 11. The detailed source derivation and proof obligations are in [StepSymPureBoundary.md](StepSymPureBoundary.md). The pure-step exact theorem remains useful for the internal Rule A/B/C iteration blocks used by `afterSweepCB`, but an outer `stepSym` bridge is still required.

## What this establishes—and what it does not

Established computationally:

1. The translated `stepSym` transition reproduces every literal upstream checkpoint for both machines.
2. Both replayed symbolic traces reach the source's final HALT checkpoint at the stated macro counts.
3. The complete trajectories have the recorded finite maxima for run fields, repeat counters, decoded tape length, and symbolic-token count.

Not established:

- a primitive TM-step count for the complete `stepSym` trace;
- a complete primitive halting time for F424 or F412;
- a comparison of either complete halting time with the BB(6) champion;
- any classification of either machine as a non-champion.

The measured 203-bit / 205-bit F424 bounds and 69-bit / 70-bit F412 bounds support the stronger, still-unformalized trace-specific route in [SymRunPrimitiveBound.md](SymRunPrimitiveBound.md). That route targets F424 < 2^463 and F412 < 2^193, conditional on the cost-indexed soundness theorem, the intermediate-state max-field/token-count folds, and the prefix bound. A second route is documented in [SymRunCoarseWeightPotential.md](SymRunCoarseWeightPotential.md): the combined potential W = 1 + token count + numeric-field sum targets the much looser conditional bounds F424 < 2^1,410,260 and F412 < 2^427,698. Its remaining formal tasks are the sweepBB length/numeric-sum induction (including a `stepElemBB` length bound and B-side `eatB2`/`eatB12R` mass instantiations) and the literal `afterSweepCB` dispatcher composition; the Rule A/B/C potential and cost inequalities are now explicit in the note. Neither route is a certified classification.

**Range-proof warning:** do not infer an intermediate-state bound from checkpoint endpoints alone. The replay records `maxFieldInChunk` separately from `maxFieldAtCheckpoint`, and the former is often larger. For example, F424's first chunk peaks at 419,581 while its endpoint field maximum is 349,284; the final chunk peaks at 12,464,561,897,144,001,482,331,935,594,779,263,804,489,570,179,353,031,112,091,137 while the endpoint is smaller. The Lean proof must fold over every intermediate state.
