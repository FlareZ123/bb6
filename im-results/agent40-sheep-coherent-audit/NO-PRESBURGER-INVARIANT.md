# No proper infinite ultimately periodic Sheep first-even invariant

Agent 40, R010, November 3, 2026. Intermediate mathematical corollary of Agent22's corrected coherent-orbit theorem, independently audited in README.md. It does not decide Sheep or BB(6).

**Theorem.** Let I be an infinite subset of positive even integers. Suppose (a) I is ultimately periodic, so above some threshold N its membership depends only on a fixed residue modulo an even m, and (b) every actual legal generic first-even Sheep return from an integer in I remains in I. Then I contains every sufficiently large even integer.

**Proof.** Let S be the set of included even residues modulo m above N. Infinitude makes S nonempty. Fix r in S and arbitrary even residue s. The coherent-orbit theorem constructs arbitrarily large x congruent r, with one genuine finite sequence of consecutive generic first-even returns ending at z congruent s. Choose x>=N. Then x belongs to I; actual forward closure puts z in I. Every generic Sheep return strictly increases its input, so z>=N, and eventual periodicity implies s in S. Since s was arbitrary, every even residue belongs to S, and all sufficiently large even integers belong to I.

**Presburger corollary.** One-variable Presburger-definable natural-number sets are ultimately periodic (see https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/dichotomy-for-kautomatic-expansions-of-presburger-arithmetic/8B56A89B78B1EF1DC6BDA34066186F5D). Therefore no one-variable Presburger-definable predicate on the current Sheep even-state integer can simultaneously contain the genuine blank entry 10, be forward inductive for every nonterminal first-even return, and exclude all halting powers of two. Otherwise its forward closure from 10 would be infinite: each safe return exists and strictly increases, and the predicate excludes terminal powers. The theorem then forces all sufficiently large even integers into the predicate, including arbitrarily large forbidden powers, a contradiction.

**Scope.** The statement concerns univariate finite-congruence/linear-threshold predicates, including any finite exceptions and quantifiers expressible in Presburger arithmetic. It does not exclude richer predicates using multiplication, unbounded history, tape state or growing moduli. Its starting numbers are freely chosen large representatives, never the exact blank entry 10.
