## General crossing-splice certificate

Run from blank for `entry` ordinary moves to state A scanning blank at `entryHead`, with the tape on and to the left of the head blank. Translate the head to zero and replay `period` assigned moves to state A at `shift`, again with the tape on and to the left blank. Each selected interval (a,b] lies strictly right of both endpoint heads, and the chronological edge-crossing sequences at a and b agree. Intervals are pairwise disjoint (adjacency allowed).

The cell-transcript splicing lemma reconstructs a legitimate computation when every selected interval is duplicated any `n` times. Each duplicated cell inherits the same local transition constraints and synchronized crossing events; no unassigned transition is used. Its input and output words become formal products with variable exponents. Crucially, the checker establishes an **exact identity for all n**, not equality of a few sampled strings.

For a right word `w`, let `P_w(n)` denote expansion by n additional copies. The normalized formal word consists of alternating fixed constants and blocks raised to the power n. To compare the finish word at its translated head, normalize both words using `x(Vx)^n = (xV)^n x`, an identity justified by induction at every n including zero. Adjacent shifted constants are absorbed into the final fixed suffix. The checked identity is `P_start(n+1)=P_finish(n)`.

Writing `C(n,h)` for the input configuration with head h, the certified recurrence is `C(n,h) → C(n+1,h+shift)` in `period+increment*n` ordinary moves, starting from `C(0,entryHead)` after `entry` ordinary moves. Every recurrence has positive cost; hence its concatenation yields arbitrarily long live prefixes, proving `NeverHalts` for every completion. The general script soundness, word normalization, recurrence and never-halts bridge are source-checked in [CrossingCore](CrossingCore.lean), [CrossingBank](CrossingBank.lean) and [CrossingSource](CrossingSource.lean). See the [worked leaf 156](README.md) for literal entry, cuts, exact word recurrence and cost.
