The previously source-proved `guard6(xs)` checks that all three successive finite-prefix weights, with offsets 509, 290 and 219 respectively, have **nonzero remainder modulo 64**. It suffices to prove `P(xs++S)≠none` for *every* suffix `S` whenever `xs.length=6`; exactly **956 of 1,024** cyclic phases satisfy this test.

**New exact sharpness theorem** (source-research candidate):

```text

```

In particular, each of the **68 residual phases** admits a length-one synthetic suffix `[t]` such that `P(prefixRun(i,phase)++[t])=none`, with `0≤t≤15897`. Their first failing sub-drain stages are 11 at `G(509)`, 18 at `G(290)`, and 39 at `G(219)`. The existence is all that is claimed; minimality of these witnesses is not a theorem.

This theorem is about **universal safety over arbitrary completions of a six-digit prefix**. It deliberately does not imply that a synthetic halting suffix is ever generated by the genuine blank-root orbit.
