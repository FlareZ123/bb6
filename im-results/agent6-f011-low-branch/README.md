# F011 (#464): exact low-branch return map

**Status:** intermediate mathematical result. This is an exact reduction of one branch of the accelerated orbit, not a proof that the machine halts or does not halt.

## Source and root

The machine is
\[
\texttt{1RB0RC\_1LC1RE\_1RA1LD\_0LC0LA\_1RF1RD\_---0RD}.
\]
The source-pinned upstream Lean module is [\`M_1RB0RC_1LC1RE_1RA1LD_0LC0LA_1RF1RD_HHH0RD.lean\`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/cryptids/blob/c86f15861709b76042998c5211c7b2f83991621b/BusyBeaver/BB6/M_1RB0RC_1LC1RE_1RA1LD_0LC0LA_1RF1RD_HHH0RD.lean).

That module defines the accelerated state \(S(p,k,c)\), starts at \(S(0,2,0)\) after ten blank-tape steps, and proves
\[
\operatorname{IsHalt}(\text{target})
\iff
\operatorname{CollatzHalts}(\text{highLevelNext},\text{highLevelStart}).
\]
Write \(k=3q+r\), with \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), and put \(d=c-q\). Its first high-level branch is
\[
c<q \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
S(p,3q+r,c)\mapsto S(p,3q+r-3c-2,4c+5).
\]

## Three-step return identity

While this branch continues to apply, the quotient and residue after one step are:

| Initial \(r\) | \(q'\) | \(r'\) | \(c'\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | \(q-c-1\) | 1 | \(4c+5\) |
| 1 | \(q-c-1\) | 2 | \(4c+5\) |
| 2 | \(q-c\) | 0 | \(4c+5\) |

Composing three such transitions gives, independently of the starting residue,
\[
\boxed{(q,r,c)\mapsto(q-21c-32,\ r,\ 64c+105).}
\]
The Boolean phase \(p\) is unchanged.

For clarity, the quotient subtractions over the three steps are:
- \(r=0\): \((c+1)+(4c+5+1)+(16c+25)=21c+32\);
- \(r=1\): \((c+1)+(4c+5)+(16c+25+1)=21c+32\);
- \(r=2\): \(c+(4c+5+1)+(16c+25+1)=21c+32\).

The corresponding \(c\)-values are \(c,\ 4c+5,\ 16c+25,\ 64c+105\).

## Closed form after complete blocks

Let \((q_n,r,c_n)\) be the state after \(n\) complete three-transition blocks, all of whose individual transitions satisfy the \(c<q\) guard. Then
\[
c_{n+1}=64c_n+105,\qquad
q_{n+1}=q_n-21c_n-32,\qquad r_n=r_0.
\]
Solving these recurrences gives
\[
\boxed{c_n=\frac{(3c_0+5)64^n-5}{3}},
\]
\[
\boxed{q_n=q_0-\frac{(3c_0+5)(64^n-1)}9+3n}.
\]
In particular, with \(\Delta_n=c_n-q_n\),
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_n=
\frac{(3c_0+5)(4\cdot64^n-1)-15-9q_0-27n}{9}.
}
\]

The within-block gap \(c-q\) strictly increases. Starting from gap \(\Delta=c-q\), its values after one and two low-branch transitions are:

| Starting residue | After one transition | After two transitions | After three transitions |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | \(\Delta+4c+6\) | \(\Delta+20c+32\) | \(\Delta+84c+137\) |
| 1 | \(\Delta+4c+6\) | \(\Delta+20c+31\) | \(\Delta+84c+137\) |
| 2 | \(\Delta+4c+5\) | \(\Delta+20c+31\) | \(\Delta+84c+137\) |

Thus an episode in the \(c<q\) branch has a unique first exit; it cannot re-enter that branch without first taking one of the other high-level transitions.

## Exact arithmetic conditions for a halt at the first exit

The pinned source map has precisely these high-level halting cases:
\[
(p,r,d)=(0,2,1)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(p,r,d)=(1,2,2).
\]
Put \(s=1\) when \(p=0\), and \(s=2\) when \(p=1\). Since \(p\) is unchanged throughout a \(c<q\) episode, a halt at its first exit can occur only at the following residue-specific crossing.

| Starting residue \(r_0\) | Exit must occur after | Exit gap \(d_{\rm exit}\) | Required equality |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 2 transitions after \(n\) complete blocks | \(\Delta_n+20c_n+32\) | \((3c_0+5)(64^{n+1}-1)=9q_0+27n+27+9s\) |
| 1 | 1 transition after \(n\) complete blocks | \(\Delta_n+4c_n+6\) | \((3c_0+5)(16\cdot64^n-1)=9q_0+27n+21+9s\) |
| 2 | At a complete-block boundary | \(\Delta_n\) | \((3c_0+5)(4\cdot64^n-1)=9q_0+27n+15+9s\) |

Each equality must be accompanied by the corresponding first-exit inequalities: all earlier states in the episode must have \(c<q\), and the displayed exit gap must be nonnegative. The equalities alone do not assert that a candidate \(n\) is the first exit.

These are exact necessary-and-sufficient arithmetic tests for a halt occurring at the first \(r=2\) exit of a \(c<q\) episode. They do not classify halts reached through a transition that starts in the \(c\ge q\) branches.

## What this reduces, and what remains open

The difficult part is no longer the repeated \(c<q\) transition itself: its complete-block evolution is explicit. The unresolved root problem is to characterize the episode-start parameters \((p,q_0,r_0,c_0)\) generated by the other branches, then determine whether any of the three displayed equations can hold at the first exit.

The source theorem transfers a proof that the high-level orbit avoids both halting cases into a nonhalting proof for the original blank-tape machine. Conversely, finding one valid first-exit equality with its guard inequalities would identify a halting orbit segment.

No finite simulation is used as evidence for either outcome here. No conclusion about F011's halting status is claimed.
